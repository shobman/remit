remit VERSION 0.3.19

For the AI practitioner

Let AI keep working without you.

Everyone monitors AI's token count and billing. Nobody monitors the scarcest resource of all: the attention of the person who must constantly judge, decide and own the work. remit records the work and lets it continue or wait without you. It only needs you for the judgement that is genuinely yours, and it will not nag you.

The shipping problem

AI can produce code faster than you can judge it.

Generated work needs clear boundaries and a person willing to review and own it.

AI opens doors and rarely closes them

It notices more work, starts more work, and leaves the unfinished part for you to carry. One request becomes a queue of open decisions.

Review cannot keep up with generation

Code can be produced faster than anyone can review and ship it. The decision to ship still lands with you.

Every decision costs you

A long queue of decisions makes it harder to keep judging well, and time on task adds to the strain.

The scarcest resource is your attention

Work starts and stops with you. The judgement stays yours.

Work starts with you, not when the AI notices something. Work stops with you, not when the AI thinks it is done. It asks you when your judgement is needed.

Your brief starts the work

The brief is in your words, filed as you said it, with one word for how far the work may go before it waits for you. remit keeps the brief as you wrote it.

It waits when it needs your answer

A question only you can answer, a rule of yours that says hold, or a run that keeps failing: each is one row on the record, waiting. A question is the one stop that reaches you where you are, as one line; everything else waits, and nothing proposes what to do about it.

The record keeps the context

Weeks later, in a new session or a different agent, the item reads the same. No chat is the memory for the work.

A separate evaluator judges the work

A fresh context that did not write the work checks it against your brief and your rules before you read it. Put that evaluator on a model from another maker, and it is less likely to wave through work from its own family.

It counts the attention each item used

The words you typed and the words said back to you, per session and per item, never summed into a score. Every context raised is on the record with its agent, its model, and its cost where the agent reports one.

The boundary

remit does not act for you.

You start and close every item

remit never starts work, merges a pull request, decides on your CI's result, rewrites your brief, or closes an item. Closing is yours, and closing is not accepting.

You choose every agent and model

A registry you write says which agent and model each role uses. Nothing runs elsewhere, nothing falls back when a seat refuses, and every refusal happens before anything is billed.

You can stop it with one command

One command ends a running chain and every context it raised, and leaves the item at its last completed stage. A context starts with an emptied environment: no token or key crosses, and its CLI logs in as you through its own configuration directory.

Bounded loops

Every loop stops itself, and your rulings become the rules.

The build loop stops itself

A builder gets four rounds at most. The same must-fix twice stops it. A must-fix that comes back after being displaced stops it, because the brief is arguing with itself, and that is yours to settle.

The judgement loop stops itself

A conductor may answer a question for you only under an elevation you wrote, twice per item; the third is yours, and a question it already answered is yours when it returns.

Your rulings become the rules

A retro you call turns the rulings you made into rubrics in the gate files: what carries an item on, what holds it for your eyes, what sends it back, what accepts a finding. The evaluators judge by your rules, and the rules improve as you rule.

One item, end to end

The board shows where every item is.

Four stages, three approval gates. You say how far an item may go; it stops there, or earlier where it needs your judgement, and the record says which. An item can depend on other items, and waits at the start until what it depends on has landed. Each row of the board is one item: attention, stage, why it stopped, the item's name and title. Beneath the row, the decision waiting for you.

One command records your answer in your words and the chain resumes where it stopped. A conductor may answer for you only under an elevation you wrote, at most twice per item; the third is yours.

Your existing AI subscriptions

Use the agents you already pay for.

Open whichever one you feel like; the work item and its state are the same. The builder and the evaluator are separate contexts, and they can be different model families: a model favours its own work, so an evaluator from another maker is less likely to wave it through.

Claude Code

Cannot spawn its own agents: every context comes through remit's one seam. It runs on your machine under your login, with no enforced read boundary.

Codex

Runs inside a sandbox limited to its own files, checked after every run and refused on a downgrade. The one seat here with an enforced boundary.

Devin

May use only the tools on a list written for the run; anything else ends it. An unknown model is turned away before the run starts. No enforced read boundary.

Copilot CLI

Cannot run git or gh in any wrapping, and that is tested before each real run. Checks the model and the reasoning effort before it bills anything. No enforced read boundary.

Pi

The odd one out: it runs local models on your own machine, and any hosted provider you have configured, with built-in tools only and no extensions. A model the machine's own catalogue does not list is refused. No enforced read boundary.

Nothing raised by remit carries your API keys or tokens; each CLI logs in as you through its own configuration directory. The README states the boundary in full, and where each CLI lists its models.

Install

Install remit in your repository.

Three scripts, the conventions, one section of AGENTS.md, a CONTRIBUTING.md it manages, a pre-push guard and the hook registrations, and a folder for the work, every file recorded in a manifest. There is no service to operate. The README explains how to inspect or pin the installer before you run it.
